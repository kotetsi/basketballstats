### Founding

Olympiacos' basketball section was founded in 1931, six years after the birth of the red-and-white Piraeus club. For decades its home was the SEF (Peace and Friendship Stadium) in Neo Faliro, one of the loudest arenas in Europe, while during its renovation the team is temporarily hosted at the SUNEL Arena. Olympiacos' identity on the court is the identity of the port: tough defence, intensity, passion and a crowd that rarely lets the team relax.

### European titles

Olympiacos have won the EuroLeague four times: in 1997, 2012, 2013 and 2026. The first trophy came in Rome, with the Piraeus side beating Barcelona to complete a landmark year, having also won the league and the Cup that same season to complete the first Greek treble. In 2012 in Istanbul they came from behind against CSKA Moscow to win 62-61 in one of the most dramatic finals in the competition's history, and a year later in London they beat Real Madrid 100-88. The fourth title was written in Athens in May 2026, again against Real, 92-85, with Evan Fournier named Final Four MVP. It was the fifth final between the two teams.

The club has reached ten EuroLeague finals and made 15 Final Four appearances, including five in a row from 2022 to 2026. They lost the finals of 1994, 1995, 2010, 2015, 2017 and 2023. Their collection also includes an Intercontinental Cup, in 2013.

### Domestic titles

As of 2026 Olympiacos have 16 Greek league titles and 12 Cups. Their first great domestic period was the five years from 1993 to 1997, when they won five league titles in a row. More recently they took the titles of 2022, 2023, 2025 and 2026, the last of which came in a fifth game of the finals against Panathinaikos, completing a year with both the EuroLeague and the league.

### Legends

- **Vassilis Spanoulis**: the leader of the 2012 and 2013 titles, MVP at both Final Fours and the face of an entire era.
- **Georgios Printezis**: his basket at the end of the 2012 final went down in history; one of the club's greatest standard-bearers.
- **Theo Papaloukas**: the tall point guard who returned to Piraeus and brought his experience to the 2012 trophy.
- **David Rivers**: the American guard who was named MVP of the 1997 Final Four in Rome.
- **Panagiotis Fasoulas**: the big man of the "golden" 1990s, pivotal in the five straight league titles and the first EuroLeague.
- **Georgios Sigalas**: a steady presence in the same generation, with personality and defensive toughness.
- **Kyle Hines**: the "undersized" centre whose energy made him irreplaceable in the 2012 and 2013 titles.
- **Kostas Papanikolaou**: a young protagonist in the 2012 and 2013 titles and later one of the team's leaders.
- **Sasha Vezenkov**: EuroLeague regular-season MVP in 2023, one of the most efficient scorers in the competition.

### Coaches

Dušan Ivković is the man who changed the club's history twice: he delivered the 1997 treble and returned to win the 2012 EuroLeague with a revamped, young team. Georgios Bartzokas succeeded the Serbian and won the 2013 trophy, while in his second spell he led Olympiacos to the 2026 triumph in Athens. Ioannis Sfairopoulos, with the league titles of 2015 and 2016 and two European finals, kept the team at the top during a transitional period.

### Today

Olympiacos enter every EuroLeague season as one of the main contenders. Stability in the boardroom, on the bench and in the core of the roster, combined with their regular Final Four appearances, has made them a benchmark for consistency. With a fourth title in their luggage and the return to the renovated SEF on the horizon, the Reds now aim not just to be in the fight for the top, but to build an era of dominance.
