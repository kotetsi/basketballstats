### Founding

Hapoel Tel Aviv was founded in 1935 as a section of the Hapoel sports organisation, which was linked to the Histadrut, Israel's great labour federation. From the start it had a popular, working-class character and a fan base that saw it as a counterweight to Maccabi, its eternal rival from the same city. For 25 years, from 1980 to 2005, its home was the small but legendary Ussishkin Street arena, which was demolished in 2007 despite a fan campaign to save it. Today its home is listed as the Menora Mivtachim Arena, with more than 10,000 seats.

### European titles

For decades Hapoel's European history was limited to a few good runs: Korać Cup semi-finals in 1980 and 1988 and a FIBA Europe League (later the EuroChallenge) semi-final in 2004. Everything changed in the 2024-25 season. With Dimitris Itoudis as coach, the team reached the EuroCup final and beat Gran Canaria 2-0, winning the second game 103-94 in the Canary Islands. It was the first European title in the club's history and brought with it a place in the EuroLeague for the 2025-26 season. In its first season in the modern EuroLeague, Hapoel finished sixth and were eliminated 3-1 in the quarter-finals by Real Madrid.

### Domestic titles

As of 2026 Hapoel have five Israeli league titles, all from their golden era: 1960, 1961, 1965, 1966 and 1969. They also have four Israeli Cups, in 1962, 1969, 1984 and 1993. The other side of their history is the more than 20 times they have finished runners-up, mostly behind Maccabi. In 2025 the league was not completed because of the war with Iran, while in 2026 the team reached the finals but lost 3-1 to Maccabi. They have reached the League Cup final twice, in 2022 and 2025, without winning it.

### Legends

Haim Hazan was the face of the team from 1953 to 1966, and later also sat on its bench. Zvi Lubetzky and Gershon Dekel were key players in the league titles of the 1960s. Barry Leibowitz, with two spells up to 1982, and Mark Turenshine were among the big names of the following years. Pini Hozez stood out in the 1970s and 1980s. American LaVon Mercer was the team's star in the big derbies against Maccabi in the 1980s, as was Amos Frishman. Nate Robinson stayed only briefly in 2016, but still found time to score 46 points in a play-off game against Hapoel Jerusalem. In the modern era Matan Naor became a symbol of the return to the top flight, while Bar Timor is the Israeli leader of the team that won the EuroCup. Jonathan Motley was the finals MVP of that competition.

### Coaches

Haim Hazan, a legend as a player, also spent time on the team's bench. Dimitris Itoudis, however, is the man who changed its history: he delivered the EuroCup, was named Israel's coach of the year in 2025 and became only the fourth coach to have won both the EuroLeague and the EuroCup.

### Today

Hapoel's history also includes a fall that few clubs could survive. After successive relegations and financial collapse in the mid-2000s, the fans created their own team from the fifth division and in 2012 brought it back to the top flight. Today, with Ofer Yannay as owner and the supporters' association holding a minority stake, Hapoel now have the financial muscle to stand alongside Europe's big clubs. After an impressive first year in the EuroLeague, they aim to establish themselves among the competition's front-runners.
