### Founding

Partizan was founded on 4 October 1945 in Belgrade, as a section of the sports club of the same name that was born after the Second World War. The "Black-and-Whites" have tied their name to the legendary Yugoslav school of basketball: technique, discipline, quick thinking and an inexhaustible supply of talent that sprang from their academies. The team's historic home was the Pionir Hall, while its big European games are now played at the Belgrade Arena, where its fanatical crowd creates one of the most spectacular atmospheres in Europe.

### European titles

The high point of Partizan's history came in 1992, when they won the EuroLeague at the Final Four in Istanbul. After beating Philips Milano in the semi-final, the Serbs overcame Joventut 71-70 thanks to a buzzer-beating three-pointer from Aleksandar Đorđević, one of the most famous baskets in the competition's history. The achievement was all the greater because, due to international sanctions, the team had played its "home" games in Fuenlabrada, Spain.

Partizan also have three Korać Cups in their collection, from 1978, 1979 and 1989. In all, they have reached the Final Four of Europe's top competition four times: in 1988 in Ghent, in 1992, in 1998 in Barcelona and in 2010 in Paris.

### Domestic titles

As of 2026 Partizan have 22 national league titles: five in Yugoslavia, eight in the joint Serbia and Montenegro league and nine in Serbia, the last in 2025. Among them is an impressive run of 13 consecutive titles from 2002 to 2014. They also have 16 national cups and eight Adriatic League (ABA) titles, in 2007, 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2013, 2023 and 2025. In 2026 they reached the ABA finals again, where they were beaten by Dubai.

### Legends

- **Dragan Kićanović**: the explosive guard of the first two Korać Cups in the late 1970s.
- **Dražen Dalipagić**: the unstoppable scorer of the same era, now in the Naismith Hall of Fame.
- **Vlade Divac**: the big man who rose to prominence at Partizan before his great NBA career, also a Hall of Fame member.
- **Aleksandar Đorđević**: the leader and executioner of 1992, with the three-pointer that delivered the EuroLeague.
- **Predrag Danilović**: the gifted scorer who flanked Đorđević in the European title-winning team.
- **Željko Rebrača**: the centre of the same generation, who later became one of the best big men in Europe.
- **Nenad Krstić**: the big man who stood out in the early 2000s and moved from Belgrade to the NBA.
- **Bogdan Bogdanović**: the guard who shone in the black-and-white shirt in the early 2010s, before Fenerbahçe and the NBA.

### Coaches

Željko Obradović began his coaching career on the Partizan bench and won the 1992 EuroLeague in his first season, returning decades later to the club that made his name. Duško Vujošević is the most successful coach in the team's history, with 23 trophies across four spells and a leading role in the long domestic dominance of the 2000s. From an earlier era, Ranko Žeravica, Aleksandar Nikolić and Dušan Ivković stand out, all members of the FIBA Hall of Fame.

### Today

In recent years Partizan have returned to the EuroLeague map with ambitious rosters and a home court that every opponent has to reckon with. Their fans, who regularly fill the Belgrade Arena, are perhaps their greatest weapon. The goal is to turn this momentum into a regular presence in the play-offs and, at some point, a return to the Final Four for the first time since 2010, while continuing the battle with eternal rivals Crvena Zvezda.
