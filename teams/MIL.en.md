### Founding

Olimpia Milano was founded in 1936 and is the most successful club in the history of Italian basketball. Over the decades it changed its name many times depending on the sponsor, and older fans remember it as Simmenthal, Billy, Tracer or Philips, before its name became linked with the house of Armani. Since 1990 its home has been the Forum arena in Assago, today known as the Unipol Forum, with a capacity of more than 12,000. The red shoes, the famous "Scarpette Rosse", became the symbol of a team that in Italy always carries the label of favourite.

### European titles

Milano first rose to the top of Europe in 1966, when they won the European Champions Cup by beating Slavia Prague in the final. The following year they returned to the final but lost to Real Madrid, while in 1983 they lost the trophy by just one point to neighbours Cantù. Their great era came at the end of the 1980s: in 1987 and 1988 they won two consecutive EuroLeagues, beating Maccabi Tel Aviv in the final both times. In 1987 they also added the Intercontinental Cup, beating Barcelona.

The collection is completed by three Cup Winners' Cups (1971, 1972, 1976) and two Korać Cups (1985, 1993). In the modern EuroLeague, Armani reached the 2021 Final Four in Cologne, where they lost the semi-final to Barcelona by two points and took third place against CSKA Moscow. That followed a third place in 1992.

### Domestic titles

No one in Italy comes close to Milano. Today they have 32 league titles, 9 Italian Cups and 7 Super Cups, the most recent in September 2026 against Venezia. The 2025-26 season was one of the most successful in the club's history: the team won the Super Cup in September 2025, the Cup in February 2026 with an 85-77 win over Tortona and the league in June, beating Venezia 3-1 in the finals. It was their fourth Scudetto in the last five years, after the titles of 2022, 2023 and 2024.

### Legends

- **Bill Bradley**: the American who, before becoming an NBA star and later a senator, led Milano to their first European title in 1966.
- **Dino Meneghin**: the greatest Italian player of all time, a pillar of the two EuroLeagues of 1987 and 1988.
- **Mike D'Antoni**: the "Arsène Lupin" of Italian basketball, the team's brains for more than a decade.
- **Bob McAdoo**: a former NBA MVP, he gave Milano the scoring they needed for the two European trophies.
- **Roberto Premier**: the Italian scorer who was a key cog in Peterson's team.
- **Danilo Gallinari**: played for Milano before the big leap to the NBA and became the symbol of a new generation.
- **Sergio Rodríguez**: the Spanish point guard who brought the team back to the Final Four in 2021.
- **Kyle Hines**: the multiple EuroLeague champion who brought experience and defence to Milano at the start of the new cycle of success.
- **Shavon Shields**: consistently one of the team's most productive players in the Scudetti of the 2020s.

### Coaches

Cesare Rubini is the club's patriarch: as a player and above all as a coach he was associated with dozens of titles and with the first European Champions Cup in 1966. Dan Peterson built the team of the 1980s and completed his work with the 1987 treble. Ettore Messina, who took over in 2019, brought Milano back to the Final Four and led them to three consecutive league titles, before resigning in November 2025. He was succeeded by his assistant, Peppe Poeta, who wrote his own chapter by winning the 2026 Cup and league title.

### Today

Armani are one of the steady pillars of the modern EuroLeague. They have one of the biggest budgets in the competition, but in recent years they have struggled to carry their domestic dominance over into Europe. With Poeta on the bench and the momentum of a domestic triple crown, the goal is clear: to get back among the eight best teams on the continent and compete for a Final Four place, where their history says they belong.
