### Founding

Anadolu Efes was founded in 1976 in Istanbul as Efes Pilsen, a team tied from day one to the well-known brewery that still backs it today. It started out in the second division, taking the place of the bankrupt Kadıköyspor. It went up unbeaten in 1978 and won the league in its very first season in the top flight. It took its current name in 2011, when alcohol advertising in sport was banned in Turkey. Unlike the city's big multi-sport clubs, Efes is a purely basketball club, something that has given it consistency and long-term planning. For years it played at the Sinan Erdem Dome, while since 2024 its home has been the new Basketball Development Center, with a capacity of around 10,000.

### European titles

Efes were the first Turkish club to win a European trophy in a team sport. That happened in 1996 in the Korać Cup, with the team beating Stefanel Milano in the final 76-68 and 70-77, in other words by just one point on aggregate over the two games. Three years earlier, in 1993, they had reached the Cup Winners' Cup final in Turin, where they lost 50-48 at the death to Aris.

In the EuroLeague they gave their first serious showing in 2000 and 2001, with two consecutive third places at the Final Four. The real breakthrough came at the end of the 2010s. In 2019 they reached the final in Vitoria, where they were beaten by CSKA Moscow. Two years later, in 2021 in Cologne, they beat Barcelona to win their first EuroLeague, and in 2022 in Belgrade they defeated Real Madrid to become back-to-back European champions. They were one of the few teams of the modern era to successfully defend their title.

### Domestic titles

In Turkey, Efes are the most successful basketball club. They have 16 league titles, the most recent in 2023, while in the years of their European triumphs they also added titles in 2019 and 2021. They also have twelve Turkish Cups, the last in 2022, and 14 Presidential Cups, the Turkish Super Cup, the most recent in 2024. In the 2024-25 and 2025-26 seasons the league title went to Fenerbahçe, with Efes eliminated in the semi-finals both times.

### Legends

Petar Naumoski was the maestro of the Korać team and his number 7 shirt has been retired. Mirsad Türkcan began at Efes the journey that took him to the NBA, as did Hedo Türkoğlu and Mehmet Okur, members of the teams that went to the 2000 and 2001 Final Fours respectively. Ufuk Sarıca was the home-grown star of the 1990s generation. Krunoslav Simon, whose number 44 has also been retired, was the experience and composure of the two-title team. Vasilije Micić was EuroLeague MVP in 2021 and Final Four MVP in both years, while Shane Larkin, the team captain, was the scorer who decided countless games. Finally, Bryant Dunston provided defence and stability in the paint throughout that run.

### Coaches

Aydın Örs built the first great Efes, with league titles, Cups and the 1996 Korać. Oktay Mahmuti kept the team at the top in Turkey in the early 2000s. Ergin Ataman, however, is the man identified with the golden era: across three spells he led the team to the 2000 Final Four, the 2019 final and the two titles of 2021 and 2022, before continuing his career at Panathinaikos.

### Today

After Ataman's departure Efes entered a phase of renewal, but they remain one of the steady pillars of the EuroLeague, with a strong budget and a place among the clubs holding long-term licences. In 2025, as sixth in the regular season, they took Panathinaikos to a fifth game in the play-offs, proof that they can still stand up to any opponent. The 2025-26 season, however, was a difficult one: Pablo Laso took over in December, the team finished 19th and Larkin left in the summer. The goal is clear: to return to where they were just a few years ago, the Final Four.
