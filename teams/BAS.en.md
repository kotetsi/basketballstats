### Founding

Baskonia was founded in 1959 in Vitoria-Gasteiz as Club Deportivo Vasconia and within a few decades transformed itself from a team from a small city in the Basque Country into one of the most consistent forces in European basketball. Fans have known it under many sponsorship names, from Taugrés and TAU Cerámica to Caja Laboral, but its identity has stayed the same: hard work, smart scouting and teams that play with passion. Its home is the Fernando Buesa Arena, with a capacity of more than 15,000. Baskonia is also known as a "nursery" of stars who took their step towards the NBA here.

### European titles

Baskonia's only European title came in 1996, when they won the Cup Winners' Cup by beating PAOK in the final, which was played in Vitoria. The EuroLeague, however, remained the great unfulfilled ambition. In 2001 the team reached a finals series against Virtus Bologna and lost it in the fifth game (2-3), while in 2005 they lost the final of the Moscow Final Four to Maccabi Tel Aviv.

The period from 2005 to 2008 is the most consistent in the club's history, with four consecutive Final Fours. After the 2005 final came semi-final exits in 2006 to Maccabi, in 2007 to Panathinaikos and in 2008 to CSKA Moscow. The fifth and most recent appearance came in 2016 in Berlin, where the team was knocked out by Fenerbahçe in the semi-final.

### Domestic titles

In Spain, Baskonia have repeatedly managed to break the Real Madrid–Barcelona monopoly. They have 4 ACB league titles (2002, 2008, 2010, 2020), 7 Copas del Rey and 4 Super Cups, the latter all in a row from 2005 to 2008. The 2020 title was won at the special tournament in Valencia, in the pandemic season. The most recent trophy came in February 2026, when Baskonia beat Real Madrid 100-89 in the Cup final in Valencia and celebrated the seventh Copa del Rey in their history, 17 years after the previous one.

### Legends

- **Luis Scola**: the Argentine big man who rose to prominence in Vitoria and became a symbol of the golden-era team; his number 4 shirt has been retired.
- **Pablo Prigioni**: the on-court brain, whose intelligence and defence set the rhythm of the Final Four years.
- **Igor Rakočević**: one of the EuroLeague's top scorers of his era, with his number 8 never to be worn again.
- **Tiago Splitter**: grew up as a player at Baskonia and left for the NBA as one of the best big men in Europe.
- **Sergi Vidal**: the Spanish forward who became identified with the shirt and was honoured with the retirement of his number 9.
- **Andrés Nocioni**: energy, toughness and crucial baskets in the years of the first great successes.
- **José Calderón**: passed through Vitoria before the NBA, as a member of the team that reached the 2005 final.
- **Mirza Teletović**: a scorer with huge shooting range, part of the teams that won the 2008 and 2010 league titles.
- **Tornike Shengelia**: the leader who guided Baskonia to the 2020 title.

### Coaches

Pepe Laso is the man who put the team on the map in the 1970s and 1980s. Duško Ivanović is the most iconic figure on its bench: across several spells he won three league titles (2002, 2010, 2020) and several cups, and led the team to its two EuroLeague finals. Velimir Perasović led the team to the Final Fours of 2006, 2007 and 2016, and in October 2026 returned for a fourth spell in Vitoria, succeeding Paolo Galbiati, who had delivered the 2026 Cup.

### Today

Baskonia are a EuroLeague member with a long tradition in the competition, but in recent years they have been in a rebuilding phase, with a limited budget compared to the continent's giants. Winning the Cup provided breathing space and confidence, while Perasović's return to the bench aims to restore the stability that once defined the team. The Buesa Arena remains a venue no opponent wants to visit, and Baskonia continue to look for a way back to the play-offs.
