### Founding

Maccabi Tel Aviv's basketball section was founded in 1932, within the multi-sport club that had existed since 1906. Over the years the team transcended sport and became something like a national symbol for Israel. Its home is Yad Eliyahu, now named the Menora Mivtachim Arena, a venue of around 10,000 seats renowned for the noisiest atmosphere in Europe. The yellow shirt became associated with spectacle, fast-paced play and the great American and European stars who passed through.

### European titles

Maccabi have six European champion titles. The first, in 1977 in Belgrade, 78-77 against Mobilgirgi Varese, is perhaps the most symbolic moment in their history, coming shortly after the legendary win over CSKA in Virton, Belgium. Next came 1981 in Strasbourg, with an 80-79 win over Sinudyne Bologna in the final. In 2001 they won FIBA's SuproLeague, the year Europe had two parallel competitions, beating Panathinaikos in Paris. Then came the double of 2004 and 2005, the first of the two titles won in front of their own fans at the Tel Aviv Final Four. The sixth star was added in 2014 in Milan, 98-86 after overtime against Real Madrid.

Maccabi have also lost nine finals, in 1980, 1982, 1987, 1988, 1989, 2000, 2006, 2008 and 2011, the last to Panathinaikos, a fact that shows how often they found themselves at the summit. Their collection also includes the 1980 Intercontinental Cup and the 2012 Adriatic League title.

### Domestic titles

In Israel their dominance is almost total. They have 58 league titles, the most recent in 2026, when under Oded Katash they beat Hapoel Tel Aviv 3-1 in the finals. The 2024-25 season ended without a champion, as the finals against Hapoel Jerusalem were not completed because of the security situation in the country. They have won the Israeli Cup 47 times, the two most recent in 2025 and 2026, when they beat Bnei Herzliya 109-90 in the final, and have also won more than ten League Cups.

### Legends

Tal Brody is the face of the 1977 team, and his words after the win over CSKA went down in the country's history. Miki Berkovich was the best Israeli scorer of his generation and a protagonist in 1981. Aulcie Perry was the big man who gave the 1977 and 1981 teams height and rebounding, while Kevin Magee dominated the paint in the late 1980s. Doron Jamchi was the shooter who kept the team at the top in the same era. Šarūnas Jasikevičius was the brains behind the 2004 and 2005 titles, with Anthony Parker alongside him in a leadership role and Nikola Vujčić providing solutions in the post. Derrick Sharp became known for his big shots in the same period and Tal Burstein for his dedication and defence over almost two decades.

### Coaches

Ralph Klein led the team to its first European triumph in 1977 and Rudy D'Amico to its second in 1981. Pini Gershon is the most successful coach in the club's history, with three European titles, in 2001, 2004 and 2005. David Blatt put his name to the 2014 title with a team that few considered favourites.

### Today

Maccabi remain one of the most recognisable clubs in the EuroLeague, with a huge fan base in and outside Israel. In recent years they have had to compete in unusual circumstances, often far from home, without losing their place in the competition. In the 2025-26 season they played most of their EuroLeague home games in Belgrade and finished twelfth, outside the play-in. Their goal does not change: to return to the Final Four and challenge once again for a seventh star.
