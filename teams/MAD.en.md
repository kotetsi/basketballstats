### Founding

Real Madrid's basketball section was founded in 1931 and very quickly became something more than an offshoot of the famous football club. Today it plays at the Movistar Arena in the Spanish capital and is, in terms of trophies, the most successful club in the history of European basketball. "Los Blancos" combine the philosophy of a big club, which demands victories in every competition, with a long tradition of developing talent through its academies.

### European titles

Real have won the European Champions Cup and the EuroLeague 11 times, an all-time record: in 1964, 1965, 1967, 1968, 1974, 1978, 1980, 1995, 2015, 2018 and 2023. The first golden period was the 1960s and 1970s, when the Madrid side dominated the continent. In 1995 in Zaragoza they beat Olympiacos, while the modern era brought three more titles: in Madrid in 2015, in Belgrade in 2018 against Fenerbahçe and in Kaunas in 2023, again against Olympiacos, 79-78.

The collection is completed by four Cup Winners' Cups (1984, 1989, 1992, 1997), a Korać Cup (1988), a EuroCup (2007) and five Intercontinental Cups (1976, 1977, 1978, 1981, 2015). Since 2011 the team has reached the Final Four 11 times, more than any other, while it lost finals in 2013, 2014, 2022, 2024 and 2026.

### Domestic titles

As of 2026 Real have 38 Spanish league titles, the most recent in 2024 and 2025, and 29 Copas del Rey, the last in 2024. They also have ten Spanish Super Cups. On the domestic stage the rivalry with Barcelona remains the great derby of Spanish basketball.

### Legends

- **Emiliano Rodríguez**: the club's first great star, leader of the team that dominated Europe in the 1960s.
- **Clifford Luyk**: the big man who became a symbol of the same generation and won many European Champions Cups.
- **Wayne Brabender**: the American scorer who became synonymous with Real in the 1960s and 1970s.
- **Juan Antonio Corbalán**: the brains behind the 1978 and 1980 titles.
- **Fernando Martín**: the first Spaniard in the NBA, with his number 10 shirt retired after his untimely death.
- **Dražen Petrović**: spent just one season in Madrid, but led the team to the 1989 Cup Winners' Cup.
- **Arvydas Sabonis**: the Lithuanian giant who delivered the 1995 EuroLeague and was MVP of that Final Four.
- **Felipe Reyes**: the captain of the modern era, part of the 2015 and 2018 titles.
- **Sergio Llull**: three EuroLeagues, regular-season MVP in 2017 and the title-winning basket in the Kaunas final.
- **Luka Dončić**: still a teenager, he was regular-season and Final Four MVP in 2018, before leaving for the NBA.

### Coaches

Pedro Ferrándiz is the architect of the legend, having won four European Champions Cups (1965, 1967, 1968, 1974) and 12 Spanish league titles. Lolo Sainz, who had celebrated four European titles as a player, added two more from the bench, in 1978 and 1980. In the more recent era Pablo Laso revived the team, leading it to the EuroLeague titles of 2015 and 2018. Special mention goes to Željko Obradović, who was on the bench in 1995.

### Today

Real remain one of the most consistent forces in today's EuroLeague. The combination of a strong budget, deep tradition and the ability to renew itself keeps the team permanently in the fight for the Final Four. Recent final defeats against Panathinaikos and Olympiacos do not change its status as the benchmark team against which every ambitious rival measures itself, with a twelfth title the clear objective.
