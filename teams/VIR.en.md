### Founding

The Virtus club began in 1871 as a gymnastics society, but its basketball section was created in 1927 and very quickly became its flagship. Bologna, which Italians call "Basket City", has for decades lived divided between Virtus and Fortitudo. The team's historic home was the PalaDozza, the city's legendary "Madison", while in the modern era Virtus have played at the Unipol Arena and at the temporary Virtus Arena in the Fiera district, where the new permanent arena, the Unipol Hall, is also being built. The black-and-white colours and the black "V" on the crest are synonymous with a team that settles for nothing less than titles.

### European titles

The first great European heartbreak came in 1981, when Virtus lost the European Champions Cup final to Maccabi Tel Aviv by a single point. In 1998, at the Final Four in Barcelona, Kinder won their first EuroLeague, beating AEK in the final. A year later they reached the final again, but fell to Žalgiris. In 2001 came the second trophy, through a finals series against TAU Cerámica that was decided in the fifth game (3-2). In 2002 Virtus hosted the Final Four in their home city and again reached the final, where they were beaten by Panathinaikos.

Virtus have also won the Cup Winners' Cup (Saporta) in 1990, the EuroChallenge in 2009, the Basketball Champions League in 2019 and the EuroCup in 2022 against Bursaspor, a title that reopened the EuroLeague door for them after 14 years away.

### Domestic titles

In Italy, Virtus have 17 league titles, the most recent in 2025, which came four years after the 2021 title. Their first golden era came straight after the war, with four consecutive Scudetti from 1946 to 1949, while in the 1990s they celebrated three in a row, from 1993 to 1995. To these are added 8 Italian Cups and 4 Super Cups. The 2000-01 season stands out as the greatest in their history, as the team won the treble: league, cup and EuroLeague.

### Legends

- **Roberto Brunamonti**: the captain identified with the shirt over 14 seasons and with the Scudetti of the early 1990s.
- **Renato Villalta**: a big man of quality and leadership in the Scudetti of 1979, 1980 and 1984.
- **Krešimir Ćosić**: the Croatian big man who brought Bologna a basketball intelligence far ahead of its time.
- **Predrag Danilović**: the leader of the team of the first EuroLeague, with scoring and personality that became legendary.
- **Antoine Rigaudeau**: the French point guard who gave rhythm and calm to the team of the two European trophies.
- **Manu Ginóbili**: the Argentine who starred in the 2001 treble before his great NBA career.
- **Marco Belinelli**: a son of Bologna, he returned after the NBA and contributed to the modern titles.
- **Miloš Teodosić**: the playmaker who led Virtus to the 2021 league title and the 2022 EuroCup.
- **Tornike Shengelia**: leader of the team that returned to the elite and finals MVP in the 2025 Scudetto.

### Coaches

Dan Peterson laid the foundations of the modern Virtus with the 1976 league title, while Alberto Bucci was the man behind several Scudetti in the 1980s and 1990s. Ettore Messina is the coach of the two EuroLeagues and the 2001 treble. In more recent history, Saša Đorđević stands out, with the 2019 Basketball Champions League and the 2021 title, as does Duško Ivanović, who delivered the 17th league title in 2025.

### Today

Virtus are now a regular EuroLeague member, with an owner who invests consistently and a fan base that demands the team recall its great days. After the disappointment of the 2025-26 season, when despite finishing first in the regular season they were knocked out by Venezia in the Italian league semi-finals, the club turned the page with Spaniard Àlex Mumbrú on the bench and several new faces on the roster. The goal remains the same: for Bologna to become a regular protagonist again in the battle for the play-offs and for another Final Four.
