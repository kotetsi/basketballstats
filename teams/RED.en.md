### Founding

Crvena Zvezda's basketball section was founded on 4 March 1945, a few weeks before the end of the Second World War, and from the very first moment became a point of reference for Belgrade. The Red-and-Whites play their big European games at the Belgrade Arena, in front of more than 18,000 spectators, while the historic Aleksandar Nikolić Hall remains part of their identity. This is a club built on the passion of one of the most fanatical crowds in Europe, on the rivalry with Partizan and on a school that has produced entire generations of Serbian and Yugoslav players.

### European titles

Despite its great tradition, Crvena Zvezda have never won the EuroLeague or the old European Champions Cup. Their only European trophy is the Cup Winners' Cup (later the Saporta Cup) of the 1973-74 season, when they beat Czechoslovakia's Spartak Brno 86-75 in the final in Udine. They also reached two more Cup Winners' Cup finals (1972, 1975) and two Korać Cup finals (1984, 1998), without success. In the modern EuroLeague the team has become a regular participant over the last decade, its best moment coming in 2016, when it reached the play-offs and faced CSKA Moscow. It has yet to experience a Final Four, and that remains the great objective for its fans.

### Domestic titles

At home the picture is entirely different. As of 2026 Crvena Zvezda have 24 league titles, a record for the country: 12 in Yugoslavia, including ten in a row from 1946 to 1955, three in the Serbia and Montenegro federation and nine in Serbia, the last in 2024. Their cup tally stands at 15, three Yugoslav and 12 Serbian, with six consecutive wins up to and including February 2026. Their trophy cabinet also holds seven Adriatic League (ABA) titles, the last in 2024. In the last two seasons the team missed the Serbian league final and watched others lift the ABA title.

### Legends

Nebojša Popović was the leader of the golden post-war decade, first as a player and then as a coach. Alongside him Borislav Stanković played in the first league titles, before becoming one of the most powerful figures in FIBA. Zoran Slavnić brought imagination and speed to the team that won the Cup Winners' Cup. Dragan Kapičić is the club's all-time leading scorer, with Vladimir Cvetković next. Saša Obradović stood out for his combativeness in the late 1980s and early 1990s, while Igor Rakočević is the player whose number 8 has been retired. Captain Branko Lazić holds the appearance record, with more than 800 games, and became the face of the team that dominated Serbia for almost a decade. Boban Marjanović and Nikola Kalinić were pillars of the team that dominated the ABA, and Miloš Teodosić returned to Serbia to wear the shirt with the star.

### Coaches

Aleksandar Nikolić, the "father" of Yugoslav basketball, was on the bench for the European triumph of 1974. Nebojša Popović won all ten consecutive league titles of 1946-1955 as coach, the first six as player-coach. In the modern era Dejan Radonjić stands out, the coach with the most games and the most wins in the club's history, with five ABA titles and five cups. Saša Obradović, a former player of the team, returned to the bench in October 2025 and left in May 2026, after the elimination by Partizan in the ABA semi-finals. Since June 2026 the coach has been Spaniard Ibon Navarro.

### Today

Crvena Zvezda are now a permanent presence in the EuroLeague, with one of the biggest budgets in the region and a full arena at almost every game. In recent seasons they have consistently been in the fight for the play-in and the play-offs, with the 2025-26 season ending in the play-in with a defeat to Barcelona, but the final step, a first Final Four qualification, has yet to come. With their fans filling Belgrade, the team remains one of the toughest places to play in the competition.
