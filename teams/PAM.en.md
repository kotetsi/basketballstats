### Founding

Valencia was born in the summer of 1986, when Valencia CF, the football club, decided to separate from its amateur sections and the Roig family took over the basketball team. Since then the club has had a steady backer, something rare in European basketball, and an identity tied to the city and its academy. For almost four decades its home was the legendary Fonteta, while since 2025 it has played at the modern Roig Arena, one of the most impressive venues in Europe. The team reached the top not with a single explosion but step by step, building title upon title.

### European titles

If there is one competition synonymous with Valencia, it is the EuroCup. The "Bats" have won it four times, more than any other club: in 2003 against Krka, in 2010 against Alba Berlin, in 2014 against UNICS Kazan and in 2019 once again against Alba. Before that, the team had twice reached the Saporta Cup final, in 1999 and 2002, but without lifting the trophy.

In the EuroLeague the journey was harder. For years Valencia stalled at the quarter-finals or earlier, their best moment being the 2011 play-offs, where they fell 3-2 to Real Madrid. The big turnaround came in the 2025-26 season: the team finished second in the regular season, came back from 0-2 down in the series against Panathinaikos, won the fifth game 81-64 at the Roig Arena and celebrated the first Final Four qualification in its history. In Athens they were knocked out in the semi-final by Real Madrid, but that journey put them firmly on the map of Europe's powers.

### Domestic titles

In Spain, where Real Madrid and Barcelona dominate, every trophy carries double value. Valencia have won two ACB league titles: in 2017, beating Real in the final, and in 2026, when they overcame Barcelona 3-1. Their trophy cabinet also includes a Spanish Cup, the club's first major trophy in 1998, and two Super Cups, in 2017 and 2025, the second with a 98-94 win over Real in the final.

### Legends

Nacho Rodilla is the club's own son, the point guard identified with the early years of the rise, and his number 11 shirt has been retired. Víctor Luengo, wearing 15, was a leader and scorer in the first European successes. Frenchman Antoine Rigaudeau brought Valencia the class of a European champion, while Argentine Fabricio Oberto provided strength and a clear head under the boards. Romain Sato was one of the most athletic players ever to wear the shirt. Rafa Martínez, the shooter wearing 17, and Montenegrin Bojan Dubljević, wearing 14, were key figures in the European successes of the 2010s and the first league title. Belgian Sam Van Rossom, wearing 9, was the quiet playmaker of that team. All five of these shirts have now been retired. In the most recent era Jean Montero stood out, a member of the All-EuroLeague First Team, play-offs MVP and league finals MVP in 2026, before moving to Olympiacos in the summer.

### Coaches

Miki Vuković gave the club its first trophy, the 1998 Cup. Paco Olmos, Neven Spahija, Velimir Perasović and Jaume Ponsarnau were the coaches of the four EuroCups. Pedro Martínez, however, holds a special place: he led Valencia to their first league title in 2017 and, in his second spell, to their first Final Four and second ACB title. In July 2026 he left by paying the release clause in his contract and was replaced by Xavi Albert, a product of the club's academy and until then his assistant.

### Today

Valencia are no longer the team that springs a surprise. With a new arena, stable ownership and an academy that keeps producing players, they have joined the circle of clubs that compete every year for a place in the EuroLeague's top eight. The 2026 Final Four and the league title that followed raised the bar, and now their fans are asking them to take the next step.
